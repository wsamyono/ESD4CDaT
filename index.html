Efficient System Design for Cancer Detection and Treatment

Advancing Computational Oncology through Machine Learning, Physics-Informed Neural Networks, and Interdisciplinary Life STEM Education.

About the Project

This project tackles critical computational challenges in oncology by uniting machine learning, hardware acceleration, and rigorous mathematical modeling. The research framework is built upon four major goals:

Goal 1: Modeling Cancer Growth

Building advanced machine learning algorithms using Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) to integrate physical laws and tumor dynamics constraints into predictive models.

Goal 2: Medical Image Processing

Developing computer vision workflows for automated counting and classification of cancer cells captured via built-in microscope camera systems.

Goal 3: Hardware Design

Creating specialized hardware architectures optimized to efficiently run classical and deep learning classification models in resource-limited settings.

Goal 4: Interdisciplinary Education

Pioneering new curricula—including "Mathematics for Machine Learning" and "IoT for Healthcare"—backed by undergraduate research programs.

Methodology

Our approach integrates rigorous mathematical formulations with scalable computational frameworks. For instance, tumor growth dynamics are modeled by constraining neural network loss functions with partial differential equations:

$$\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = D \nabla^2 u + r u \left(1 - \frac{u}{K}\right) - \mathcal{N}(u; \theta)$$

By enforcing these conservation laws directly into deep learning architectures, our models maintain biological interpretability and high accuracy even with sparse training datasets.
